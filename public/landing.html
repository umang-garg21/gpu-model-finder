Quantization guide

How much VRAM does a 70B model need?

Quantization is the biggest lever for fitting large models on consumer hardware. Here's exactly what each format costs for a 70B parameter model.

Format Bytes / param 70B model VRAM Quality vs FP16 Typical hardware
FP32 4 280 GB Reference 4× A100 / H100
FP16 / BF16 2 140 GB ≈ FP32 2× A100 / H100
INT8 1 70 GB Minimal loss A100 80 GB / 3× 3090
GGUF Q8 1 70 GB Near-lossless A100 80 GB / 3× 3090
INT4 / GGUF Q4 0.5 35 GB Small quality drop 2× RTX 3090 / 4090

* Actual VRAM includes ~20% overhead for KV-cache and activations. Numbers are approximate.

Quantization reduces the bit-width of model weights, dramatically cutting memory usage. INT8 (1 byte/param) cuts VRAM in half with minimal quality loss on most tasks. 4-bit (0.5 bytes/param) cuts it by 75% with a small but noticeable quality drop. For tasks like code generation or precise reasoning, FP16 or INT8 is usually preferred. For general chat, 4-bit is often indistinguishable.

Fit to Metal normalises each benchmark score against the known frontier ceiling for that benchmark, then weights by difficulty squared. Humanity's Last Exam (HLE) has a frontier ceiling of ~35% — so scoring 30% on HLE is near-frontier. MMLU has a ceiling of ~92% — scoring 80% on MMLU is mediocre by frontier standards. Raw scores are not comparable; normalised difficulty-weighted scores are. This prevents models with only easy benchmark data from outranking genuine frontier models.
